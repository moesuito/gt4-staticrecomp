`vkQueueSubmit failed: (-4: VK_ERROR_DEVICE_LOST)` at 53 s, then `OSD [GSDeviceLost]`, then `[ee] halted: GS process

**Not yet fixed:** the launcher names the loss but does not remember it; the next Play picks Vulkan again.

### 2. The launcher costs ~20% CPU in the background (biggest defect)
His Task Manager: `GT4Recomp.exe` at almost 20% of the i5 while the game ran, ~15% for the game; Windows "reduce motion"
(kills animations) dropped it below 1% and the game improved. Evidence in the F8 data: in session-1792983673 the **EE CPU**
itself is 19.61/19.16 ms (not waits), against 6-12 ms in the other runs: a 2-core U-series CPU starved by the launcher.
Cause in our UI: six full-screen background layers with `animation: bg-show 72s linear infinite, bg-pan 72s linear
infinite` (`ui/css/app.css:181`), plus `play-sheen` (3.4 s infinite) and a `setInterval` poll (`ui/pages/home.js:410`).
Hiding the window on Play (`tray.rs`) does not stop WebView2's animations. With GPU acceleration off (his test 4) the
transforms are painted on the CPU, which is why it was the same or worse.

### 3. False "crashed" on a normal close

### 4. Render scale: 100% = 1x on the integrated GPU, then 125% jumps to 4x

- **Direct3D 12 is clearly slower on this laptop** (sustained 17-20 ms walls against 16.7 on D3D11), so the "slightly
  heavier" impression is real; the GS thread busy goes to 6-13 ms. Direct3D 11 stays.
- **The limiter is the EE thread, not the GPU.** In the late intervals the EE CPU is 14-17 ms while VU1 wait, readbacks and
  download are ~0; the GPU is 20-30% busy and no thread is pegged, because the EE gates the frame by latency, not by
  saturation. A 15 W 2-core Kaby Lake at 1x is simply at its own limit in the heaviest stretches.
- **The performance overlay costs real frames** (three late intervals and the GS thread twice as busy); it stays off for
  measurements (the launcher default is off anyway).
- **Consequence:** revert the quality-costing settings (filter bilinear PS2, mipmapping on, trilinear automatic, dithering
  unscaled, preload full) and keep only the free/invisible ones (custom textures off) plus blending **Minimum** in the
  Performance Mode preset. The real lever on this machine is the launcher's background CPU (20% of a 2-core laptop), which
  is the fix already queued.
- **2x question, answered by the model and to confirm by measurement:** the EE work is resolution-independent, but 2x
  quadruples the GS work; if the GS thread falls behind, the frames-in-flight queue makes the EE block (`GS back-pressure`
  in F8). At 1x the GPU sits at 20-30%, so 2x is plausible only with the launcher idle; measure `wall`, `bp` and `GS busy`
  at 1x and 2x after the launcher fix.

## PCSX2 baseline on the same laptop, and the 2x decision (2026-10-26)

Owner ran PCSX2 itself on the i5-7200U / HD 620, same scene (Time Trial Nordschleife, Pitwork Z), 1x, Direct3D 11,
blending Minimum, **Accurate readbacks**, MTVU on, no speed hacks, clock fixed at 3.1 GHz (ThrottleStop, undervolt):

| | GT4Recomp (Performance Mode) | PCSX2 (Accurate) |
|---|---|---|
| whole-CPU use | **70-80%**, FPS dips | **~50%**, stable 60 FPS |
| GPU | 20-30% | 20-30% |
| flares | off | on |

Static recompilation should not be slower than the JIT, so those ~25 CPU points are **our overhead**: per-instruction
budget accounting and `preempt` checks in the generated code, memory/FPU/MMI helpers, and the GS IPC (~1,670 records per
frame plus wakes to a separate process). That is the gap the EE CPU work has to close. MTVU on in PCSX2 is the fair
comparison (the port also runs a VU1 worker).

**2x is closed on this laptop:** 2x puts the GPU at 60-70% and the CPU at ~80%, the package reaches the ~15 W limit
(CPU and iGPU share it) and the clock falls from 3.1 to 2.6-2.7 GHz. In both programs. The Performance Mode preset stays
at 1x; 2x is a machine-class question, not a bug.
