# 2026-10-31

## TASK-144: full-range trigger sensitivity and pedal modulation study (issue #134)

- Addressed GitHub Issue #134 ("Full Range Trigger Support", reported by @shadowcla02-cloud).
- Detailed problem analysis: players binding LT/RT analog triggers to throttle and brake experience
  an "on/off switch" sensation where the vehicle reaches 100% full throttle or maximum brake lockup
  at approximately ~35% physical trigger travel, leaving the remaining ~65% of mechanical stroke dead.
- Identified physical & architectural root cause:
  - Original PS2 DualShock 2 face/shoulder buttons were pressure-sensitive rubber pads with only 1.5–2.0 mm
    compression travel.
  - Polyphony Digital's guest input driver hardcodes full saturation (100% throttle/brake) at pressure byte
    `0x5A` (90 / 255 ≈ 35.3%).
  - Modern PC gamepads (Xbox, DualSense, Switch Pro) feature dedicated analog triggers with 8–12 mm travel
    reporting linear 0..255 values, causing premature in-game saturation at 35% trigger pull.
- Forensic analysis of community PCSX2 patch:
  - Deconstructed Silent & Aero_'s `.pnach` patch for `SCUS-97328` (addresses `0x0043BEF0`, `0x0043BF50`,
    `0x0043C010`). The patch removes the conditional bitshift/clamping in the guest EE assembly.
  - Noted why `.pnach` does not apply out-of-the-box to GT4Recomp: recompilation is 100% ahead-of-time (AOT)
    into static translation units (`unit_XXXX.cpp`) without a runtime JIT or cheat engine, and Spec II uses
    different beta ELF addresses (`SCUS-97436`).
- Established zero-binary-modification host calibration:
  - `native/controls.cpp` already implements `scale` in `read_trigger(...)`:
    `pressure = floor(min(1.0, shape(fraction, curve) * scale) * trigger_full)`
  - Applying `scale = 0.353` (90 / 255) maps 100% physical trigger stroke to pressure byte 90, matching GT4's
    saturation threshold and granting the player 100% of physical travel for fine throttle and brake modulation.
  - Can be combined with `curve=quadratic` or `curve=s_curve` for progressive low-grip modulation and trail-braking.
